From Concern to Contribution

Muslim communities worldwide endure ongoing humanitarian crises, from the displacement of Palestinians and the statelessness of the Rohingya to civil war in Sudan, the persecution of Uyghurs, and violence in Kashmir. SAFWAH’26 equips young Muslims to respond with principle and accountability.

Keynote & Panel Forums

Practitioners, advocates and scholars with direct field experience.

Students and researchers present papers and posters in three rooms.

Conference Objectives

Aligned with MADANI S.C.R.I.P.T: Sustainability, Compassion, Respect, Innovation, Prosperity and Trust.

1

Awareness

Deepen youth understanding of the crises faced by Muslim communities worldwide.

2

Responsibility

Cultivate empathy, accountability and ukhuwwah through principled responses.

3

Platform

Enable students, researchers and communities to exchange ideas and build advocacy initiatives.

Presentation format
  • Oral: 15 minutes (10 presentation + 5 Q&A), slides on room AV.
  • Poster: 15 minutes (5-minute pitch + 10 Q&A) at poster boards along room walls.
Judging criteria (100 points)
  • Content & Methodology: 40
  • Delivery & Visual Quality: 30
  • Q&A & Defense: 30
  • Two judges per room (academicians and subject experts).
Awards & publication
  • Best Speaker and Best Paper: three winners each, one per sub-theme. Best Poster: three winners.
  • Abstracts of all 30 presentations in the e-Abstract Booklet.
